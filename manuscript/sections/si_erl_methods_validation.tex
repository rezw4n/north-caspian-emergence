\subsection*{Spatial blocking}
The domain was tiled with \metric{params.block_size_km}~km squares aligned to the equal-area grid; each block takes the sector that contains its centre, which leaves \metric{blocks.n_total} blocks. They were assigned at random to training, calibration and test in proportions \metric{params.split_train}, \metric{params.split_calib} and \metric{params.split_test}, stratified by sector (seed \metric{params.seed}): \metric{blocks.n.train}, \metric{blocks.n.calib} and \metric{blocks.n.test} blocks (Supplementary Fig.~\ref{fig:studyarea}). The assignment was fixed before any evaluation, and a point or cell takes the split of its block. Every fitted quantity comes from training blocks only: the setup model and its threshold, the static-contour rule, the lidar constant, the grid offsets, the conformal scale model and the shape offsets of the hindcast audit. Conformal quantiles come from calibration blocks, and every reported accuracy and coverage from test blocks.

\emph{Block size.} Before any test-block value was read, an exponential model was fitted to the semivariogram of the topography-minus-lidar residuals in training blocks (\metric{params.vario_bin_km}~km lag bins up to \metric{params.vario_max_lag_km}~km, from at most \metric{params.vario_max_pairs} random point pairs). On synthetic white noise such a fit can return a long range, so its range is used only if spatial structure is detected (pre-registration Amendment~11). The statistic $T$ is the mean semivariance of pairs closer than \metric{params.vario_short_lag_km}~km over that of all pairs up to \metric{params.vario_max_lag_km}~km, with $p = (1 + \#\{T_{\mathrm{perm}} \le T\})/(N + 1)$ from $N = \metric{params.structure_perm_n}$ permutations of the residuals over the locations; structure is detected at $p \le \metric{params.alpha}$. If it is, and the practical range exceeds \metric{params.block_size_km}~km, the blocks grow to the next multiple of \metric{params.block_size_km}~km at or above the range and are reassigned with the same seed; with two lidar products the larger size applies. On the topography the fitted range lay beyond the largest lag, with no sill, which indicates a regional trend between sectors rather than a stationary range. By the rule fixed for that case before any calibration- or test-block value was read (pre-registration Amendment~15), the semivariogram was refitted on residuals less each sector's training median; that range also lay beyond the largest lag, so the block size was capped at \metric{h2.block_size_km}~km, the largest of the registered sensitivity sizes, and the blocks were reassigned with the same seed. At that size the Ural delta sector has no test block. The validation and conformal results use these blocks; the hindcast shape offsets, the static-contour rule and the hindcast block test keep the \metric{params.block_size_km}~km blocks on which they were fixed. Blocks of \metric{params.block_size_sens_lo_km} and \metric{params.block_size_km}~km are the block-size sensitivity of the validation (robustness check~8).

\subsection*{Accuracy of the topography}
\emph{Reference heights.} The primary reference is ICESat-2 ATL06 and ATL08 (Release~007)\citep{atl06_v7,atl08_v7,markus2017}. ATL06 is processed over all land, but its user guide notes that data outside the polar regions have not been thoroughly evaluated and may contain errors\citep{atl06_v7}. Segments were kept if their height and geoid height were finite, they came from a strong beam and their height lay within \metric{params.gross_error_m}~m of the product's own reference elevation; ATL06 segments also needed a zero quality summary, and ATL08 segments no multiple scattering and no cloud. The ATL08 water mask was not used: it is static, and would remove the very seabed under test. A point was used only if the seabed was dry when measured, that is, more than \metric{params.emergence_lag_days}~days after the emergence date of every topography cell within \metric{params.dry_radius_px_atl06} cell (ATL06) or \metric{params.dry_radius_px_atl08} cells (ATL08) of it, and the test is repeated with \metric{params.emergence_lag_sens_days}~days as a robustness check. ATL06 and ATL08 largely share photons, so they are evaluated separately and never pooled. GEDI L2A footprints\citep{gedi_l2a_v2} are an additional reference, and the substitute for ICESat-2, under the rules of pre-registration Amendment~2; GLO-30 heights over cells dry throughout its acquisition period (December 2010 to January 2015) are a further check. Both were run after the ICESat-2 decision and are reported beside it; the decision is not re-made on them. The reference population of every source is bare emerged seabed: points inside the band that was water in \metric{params.highstand_label} and not in \metric{params.lowstand_label}, in a cell whose E3 emergent-vegetation share is below \metric{params.veg_cell_share} (pre-registration Amendment~15). Land above the old shoreline, where the topography is only a triangulated extrapolation, and reed canopy are not seabed; the vegetated population is robustness check~9. The population applies to the topography and the grids alike, on the same points.

\emph{Metrics and decision rule.} Errors are $e_j = \hat{z}_j - y_j$, model minus reference, at points $j$. For the topography and each grid we report the bias (the mean of $e$), the root-mean-square error (RMSE), the normalised median absolute deviation
% src/caspian/validation.py: nmad (NMAD_SCALE = 1.4826, params.nmad_scale), rmse, bias, error_metrics
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{NMAD} = k_{\mathrm{N}} \, \operatorname*{median}_{j} \left\lvert e_j - \operatorname*{median}_{l} e_l \right\rvert ,
  \qquad k_{\mathrm{N}} = \metric{params.nmad_scale} ,
  \label{eq:si_nmad}
\end{equation}
and the \metric{params.err_pct_lo}th and \metric{params.err_pct_hi}th percentiles of $e$, pooled over test-block points where the reference and every model have a value, overall and per sector; the median of per-block values is a sensitivity. Each grid enters with one constant offset estimated on training blocks, as for the lidar constant, so that no grid is penalised for an undocumented datum; the grids as distributed are reported beside them. For a metric $M$ (NMAD or RMSE) and grid $p$, the paired difference on the same test-block points $\mathcal{P}$ and its interval are
% src/caspian/validation.py: paired_block_bootstrap (one draw of blocks serves every grid and metric),
% block_bootstrap (blocks drawn with replacement, a block drawn twice enters twice), percentile_interval
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_M = M\!\left(e^{\mathrm{DEM}}_{\mathcal{P}}\right) - M\!\left(e^{p}_{\mathcal{P}}\right) , \qquad
  \Delta_M^{(r)} = M\!\left(e^{\mathrm{DEM}}_{\mathcal{P}^{(r)}}\right) - M\!\left(e^{p}_{\mathcal{P}^{(r)}}\right), \quad r = 1, \dots, R ,
  \label{eq:si_bootstrap}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{P}^{(r)}$ pools the points of $B$ test blocks drawn with replacement from the $B$ test blocks, $R = \metric{params.bootstrap_n}$, and the interval is the central \metric{params.ci_pct}\% percentile range of the $\Delta_M^{(r)}$. H2 is supported if the topography's NMAD and RMSE are both lower than every grid's with intervals of $\Delta_M$ that exclude zero, for both ATL06 and ATL08; it is partially supported if this holds for one. Per-sector intervals in sectors with few test blocks are descriptive.

\subsection*{Conformal intervals}
Height intervals come from split conformal prediction with locally normalised scores\citep{lei2018}. With $y$ the reference height on the DAHITI reference, $\hat{y}$ the topography at the same point and $x$ the point's covariates, the score and the scale are
% src/caspian/conformal.py: scores; src/caspian/validation.py: scale_design ([1, log(1 + d), kappa, g]),
% fit_scale_model (least squares of log max(|y - yhat|, floor) on training-block points), scale_values
\begin{equation}
  r = \frac{\lvert y - \hat{y} \rvert}{s(x)} , \qquad
  s(x) = \exp\!\left(\beta_0 + \beta_1 \log(1 + d) + \beta_2\,\kappa + \beta_3\,g\right),
  \label{eq:si_score}
\end{equation}
where $d$ is the interpolation distance, $\kappa$ the density of contour points (per \kmsq{} within \metric{params.contour_density_radius_m}~m) and $g$ the slope by central differences on the \metric{params.dem_res_m}~m grid. $\beta$ was fitted by least squares of $\log \max(|y - \hat{y}|, \epsilon)$, with $\epsilon = \metric{params.abs_resid_floor_mm}$~mm, on training blocks. With $r_{(1)} \le \dots \le r_{(n)}$ the sorted scores of the $n$ usable calibration-block points and nominal coverage $1-\alpha = \metric{params.conformal_level}$, the quantile and the interval are
% src/caspian/conformal.py: conformal_quantile (k = ceil((n + 1)(1 - alpha)); inf if k > n), intervals;
% src/caspian/validation.py: calibrate, coverage_on_test
\begin{equation}
  \hat{q} = r_{(k)} , \quad k = \left\lceil (n+1)(1-\alpha) \right\rceil \quad (\hat{q} = \infty \text{ if } k > n) , \qquad
  C(x) = \left[ \hat{y} - \hat{q}\,s(x) ,\; \hat{y} + \hat{q}\,s(x) \right] .
  \label{eq:si_conformal}
\end{equation}
Coverage holds if calibration and test points are exchangeable; we assume exchangeability between blocks, which the disjoint calibration blocks are meant to make plausible. To check conditional coverage, $\hat{q}$ was computed per sector (Mondrian calibration)\citep{vovk2013,vovk2022,angelopoulos2023}: with $n_g$ the usable calibration points of sector $g$,
% src/caspian/conformal.py: mondrian_calibration (own q-hat when n_g >= min_n, else the pooled one), mondrian_q;
% min_n = design.MONDRIAN_MIN_N (params.mondrian_min_n)
\begin{equation}
  \hat{q}_g =
  \begin{cases}
    \hat{q}\ \text{from the scores of sector } g & \text{if } n_g \ge n_{\mathrm{M}} , \\
    \hat{q}\ \text{from all calibration scores} & \text{otherwise,}
  \end{cases}
  \qquad n_{\mathrm{M}} = \metric{params.mondrian_min_n} ,
  \label{eq:si_mondrian}
\end{equation}
and a test point of sector $g$ takes $C(x)$ with $\hat{q}_g$. The value, count and fallback are reported for every sector. A point is usable if its reference, prediction and scale are all present; points outside the triangulation are excluded from calibration and coverage alike, and counted. Coverage on test blocks is reported overall and per sector with its exact binomial (Clopper--Pearson) \metric{params.ci_pct}\% interval, and counts as consistent with the nominal level if \metric{params.conformal_level} lies inside it. Points within a block are correlated, which makes that interval optimistic, so a block-bootstrap interval is also reported.
