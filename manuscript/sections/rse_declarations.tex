\section*{Data availability}
The data and code that support the findings of this study are openly available at \url{https://github.com/rezw4n/north-caspian-emergence}. The repository holds the level-binned observed extents and waterlines of the North Caspian and of the two arms of Great Salt Lake, the hindcast tables, the waterline elevation model with its accuracy assessment, and every derived table behind the figures and numbers of this paper. It also holds the code that produces every result, figure, table and number from the raw inputs, under the MIT licence; one command (\texttt{make all}) rebuilds them. The pre-registration and its dated amendments are part of the repository and of the Supplementary Material.

The input data are openly available from the providers cited in Section~\ref{sec:data}: Landsat, the DAHITI and Hydroweb water levels, the five bathymetry grids, ICESat-2, GEDI and Copernicus DEM heights, SWOT, ERA5, OpenStreetMap (Open Database License), ESA WorldCover, GWL\_FCS30, the Global Lakes and Wetlands Database, the World Database on Protected and Conserved Areas, and the USGS gauge levels and 3DEP elevation data for Great Salt Lake. Gauge levels of the Coordinating Committee on Hydrometeorology of the Caspian Sea (CASPCOM) were used only to estimate the offset to the Baltic Height System. The CASPCOM catalogue states no licence, so we report derived offset statistics and their uncertainty, with the code that recomputes them from the catalogue, and do not redistribute the gauge levels \citep{caspcom2026}. Protected-area polygons are not redistributed, in line with the database's terms. Results derived from the Copernicus DEM were produced using Copernicus WorldDEM-30 \textcopyright{} DLR e.V. 2010--2014 and \textcopyright{} Airbus Defence and Space GmbH 2014--2018 provided under COPERNICUS by the European Union and ESA; all rights reserved.
