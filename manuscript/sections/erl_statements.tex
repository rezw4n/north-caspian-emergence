\section*{Data availability statement}
The data that support the findings of this study are openly available at \url{https://github.com/rezw4n/north-caspian-emergence}. They comprise the level-binned observed extents and waterlines of the North Caspian (1992--2026) and of the two arms of Great Salt Lake, the hindcast tables, the waterline elevation model with its documented accuracy, and every derived table behind the figures and numbers of this Letter. The input data are openly available from the providers cited in the Methods and the Supplementary Information: Landsat, the altimetry levels, the five bathymetry grids, ICESat-2, GEDI and Copernicus DEM heights, ERA5, OpenStreetMap (Open Database License), ESA WorldCover, the World Database on Protected Areas, and the USGS gauge levels and 3DEP elevation data for Great Salt Lake. Gauge levels of the Coordinating Committee on Hydrometeorology of the Caspian Sea (CASPCOM) were used to estimate the offset to the Baltic Height System. The catalogue states no licence, so we report derived offset statistics (per post and combined) and their uncertainty, together with the code that recomputes them from the catalogue; the catalogue's gauge levels themselves are not redistributed\citep{caspcom2026}. Protected-area polygons are not redistributed, in line with the database's terms. Results derived from the Copernicus DEM were produced using Copernicus WorldDEM-30 \textcopyright{} DLR e.V. 2010--2014 and \textcopyright{} Airbus Defence and Space GmbH 2014--2018 provided under COPERNICUS by the European Union and ESA; all rights reserved.

\section*{Code availability}
All code that produces the results, figures, tables and every number in this Letter, from the raw inputs to the text, is openly available at \url{https://github.com/rezw4n/north-caspian-emergence} under the MIT licence; one command (\texttt{make all}) rebuilds it. The repository also holds the pre-registration with its dated amendments.

\section*{Use of generative AI}
Claude Code (Anthropic; Claude Opus 5.5) was minimally used under the authors' direction to write the analysis code and review the draft. The authors set the questions, approved every decision and reviewed the results and the text; the AI tool is not an author, and the authors take full responsibility for the content.
