\subsection*{Study domain and reporting sectors}
The study domain is the North Caspian Sea north of the conventional boundary between Chechen Island and Cape Tyub-Karagan on the Mangyshlak Peninsula. Both anchor points were taken from OpenStreetMap. The domain is extended landward to contain the largest water extent of the study period, and covers \metric{study_area.domain_km2}~\kmsq{} (land included). East of the cape its southern edge runs over the Mangyshlak Peninsula, so that Mangyshlak Bay, the Buzachi coast, Komsomolets Bay and the Kaidak flats lie inside it. Results are reported for seven sectors, defined and frozen before any evaluation:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the Kizlyar Bay--Dagestan--Kalmyk coast;
  \item the Volga delta;
  \item the Volga--Ural interfluve coast;
  \item the Ural delta;
  \item the northeastern Kazakh coast (Tengiz--Kashagan);
  \item Komsomolets Bay (Mertvyi Kultuk) with the Kaidak flats;
  \item the open shelf.
\end{itemize}
The coastal sectors were drawn by eye on the transitions layer of the Global Surface Water record\citep{pekel2016}. The open shelf is the remainder; it includes the Buzachi coast and Mangyshlak Bay. To check the layout, we traced the shorelines at the maximum and the most recent levels of the \metric{levels.dahiti.smoothed.window_records}-record running median of the DAHITI series (below) in GEBCO\_2022, GEBCO\_2026, ETOPO~2022 and SRTM15+, taking the levels at face value. In these grids the coastal sectors hold \metric{study_area.layout.band_share_coastal_pct_min}--\metric{study_area.layout.band_share_coastal_pct_max}\% of the seabed between the two shorelines inside the domain. They reach \metric{study_area.layout.max_offshore_km_min}--\metric{study_area.layout.max_offshore_km_max}~km offshore of the present shoreline; the Volga delta sector reaches \metric{study_area.layout.max_offshore_km.volga.min}--\metric{study_area.layout.max_offshore_km.volga.max}~km. The domain and sector polygons are defined by their vertices in longitude and latitude. Our own code, and every area given in this paper, reads their edges as straight lines in longitude and latitude, so the box sides follow parallels and meridians. Earth Engine, which selected the scenes and mapped the active zone, reads the same edges as geodesics. The two readings differ most along the northern edge of the domain, which as a geodesic bows up to about \metric{study_area.geodesic.north_edge_offset_km}~km farther north, so that the domain encloses \metric{study_area.geodesic.domain_km2}~\kmsq{}; no other edge moves by more than \metric{study_area.geodesic.other_edge_offset_max_km}~km. In each of the grids, the seabed that changes domain or sector between the two readings is at most \metric{study_area.geodesic.band_changed_pct_max}\% of the seabed between the two shorelines inside the domain. The sector polygons, their equal-area extents and the code that defines them are part of the data release. All areas and grids use a Lambert azimuthal equal-area projection centred at \metric{params.laea_lat0_deg}\textdegree{}N, \metric{params.laea_lon0_deg}\textdegree{}E on the WGS84 ellipsoid.

\subsection*{Satellite imagery}
We used every Landsat~5, 7, 8 and 9 Collection~2 Level-2 Tier~1 surface-reflectance scene\citep{usgs_l45tm_c2l2,usgs_l7etm_c2l2,usgs_l89oli_c2l2} and every Sentinel-2 Level-2A scene (modified Copernicus Sentinel data, processed by ESA) that intersects the domain. This is \metric{inventory.n_scenes_total} scenes from \metric{inventory.first.TM_L5} to \metric{inventory.last.MSI_S2}, accessed through Google Earth Engine:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \metric{inventory.n_scenes.TM_L5} Thematic Mapper;
  \item \metric{inventory.n_scenes.ETM_L7} Enhanced Thematic Mapper Plus;
  \item \metric{inventory.n_scenes.OLI_L8} Operational Land Imager;
  \item \metric{inventory.n_scenes.OLI_L9} Operational Land Imager-2;
  \item \metric{inventory.n_scenes.MSI_S2} Sentinel-2 granules.
\end{itemize}
The scenes come from \metric{inventory.n_landsat_path_rows} Landsat path/rows and \metric{inventory.n_s2_tiles} Sentinel-2 tiles. Reflectance bands were harmonised to common names (blue, green, red, near-infrared, and the two shortwave-infrared bands) using the product scale factors.

\emph{Landsat masking.} We masked pixels flagged in the quality band\citep{usgs_l45tm_c2l2,usgs_l7etm_c2l2,usgs_l89oli_c2l2} as any of: fill, cloud, dilated cloud, cloud shadow, or cirrus (OLI only). We also masked pixels saturated in any band used. Snow and ice are treated under Water classification.

\emph{Sentinel-2 masking.} We masked pixels with a Cloud Score+ cumulative clear score below \metric{params.s2_cloudscore_threshold}\citep{pasquarella2023}.

\subsection*{Water levels}
Each image is attributed with the water level at its acquisition time.

\emph{Primary series.} The primary series is the multi-mission altimetry series of the Database for Hydrological Time Series of Inland Waters (DAHITI) for the Caspian Sea\citep{schwatke2015}: \metric{levels.dahiti.n_records} records from \metric{levels.dahiti.first} to \metric{levels.dahiti.last}. DAHITI heights are normal heights, that is, ellipsoidal altimeter heights minus a high-resolution quasi-geoid model\citep{schwatke2015}. The record maximum is \metric{levels.dahiti.max_m}~m, on \metric{levels.dahiti.date_of_max}; the minimum is \metric{levels.dahiti.min_m}~m, on \metric{levels.dahiti.date_of_min}. Both are on the DAHITI reference.

\emph{Record uncertainty.} The formal uncertainties distributed with the series are at most \metric{levels.dahiti.sigma_formal_max_m}~m, much smaller than the series' own short-term scatter. We therefore estimated the record noise $\sigma$ empirically, jointly with the diffusion rate $q$ of the interpolation model below. Each record was predicted by linear interpolation from pairs of earlier and later records spanning at most \metric{params.max_gap_days}~days. With $g_1$ and $g_2$ the time spans to the two predicting records and $w=g_1/(g_1+g_2)$, the model gives the residual a variance of $\sigma^2 f + q\,g_1g_2/(g_1+g_2)$, where $f=1+w^2+(1-w)^2$. Residuals scaled by $\sqrt{f}$ therefore have variance $\sigma^2 + q\,x$, with $x=g_1g_2/[(g_1+g_2)f]$. An iteratively reweighted least-squares fit of the robust variances (squared normalised median absolute deviations) of the scaled residuals, grouped by $x$, gives $\sigma = \metric{levels.dahiti_noise_floor_m}$~m (moving-block bootstrap standard error \metric{levels.se.dahiti_noise_floor_m}~m). Each record's 1$\sigma$ combines $\sigma$ with the formal value. Under the fitted model, \metric{levels.diag.neighbour_z_n_gt_2_5} of \metric{levels.diag.neighbour_z_n} records lie more than \metric{levels.diag.neighbour_z_threshold}$\sigma$ from their neighbours, against \metric{levels.diag.neighbour_z_expected} expected for Gaussian noise; no record was removed or down-weighted.

\emph{Gap filling.} Sampling falls from a median of \metric{levels.dahiti.records_per_year_1993_2021_median} records per year (\metric{levels.dahiti.dense_period}) to \metric{levels.dahiti.records_per_year_2022_2025_min}--\metric{levels.dahiti.records_per_year_2022_2025_max} per year in \metric{levels.dahiti.sparse_period}. For scenes outside the DAHITI record, or whose bracketing DAHITI records are more than \metric{params.max_gap_days}~days apart, the level is taken from the Hydroweb operational series for the Caspian\citep{cretaux2011,hydrowebwl}: \metric{levels.hydroweb.n_records} records to \metric{levels.hydroweb.last}. The difference between DAHITI and Hydroweb, interpolated to the \metric{levels.crosscheck.n_pairs} DAHITI epochs that have Hydroweb records within \metric{levels.crosscheck.window_days}~days on each side, has a robust scatter of \metric{levels.crosscheck.nmad_m}~m and drifts by \metric{levels.crosscheck.drift_m_per_decade}~m per decade. Hydroweb was therefore shifted by the median of the differences within $\pm$\metric{levels.gapfill.half_window_years}~years of each scene, not by their overall median (\metric{levels.crosscheck.offset_median_m}~m). Over the gap-filled scenes the shift has a median of \metric{levels.gapfill.offset_median_m}~m (range \metric{levels.gapfill.offset_min_m}--\metric{levels.gapfill.offset_max_m}~m); the overall median would have put \metric{levels.gapfill.n_scenes_other_bin_pooled_offset} of them in a different \metric{params.bin_width_m}~m level bin. The 1$\sigma$ of each Hydroweb record combines its own noise (\metric{levels.hydroweb_noise_floor_m}~m, or the distributed value where larger) with the scatter of the differences about the moving median (\metric{levels.gapfill.resid_nmad_m}~m). The error of the shift is common to all scenes filled from the same years, so it is kept separate and added after interpolation. It combines the standard error of the moving median with the year-to-year variability of the differences beyond their sampling noise in the surrounding years: a median of \metric{levels.gapfill.tau_p50_m}~m over the gap-filled scenes, and never less than the record-wide \metric{levels.gapfill.tau_m}~m. The median common 1$\sigma$ is \metric{levels.gapfill.offset_sigma_p50_m}~m. Single years depart from the moving median by up to \metric{levels.gapfill.max_year_departure.m}~m (\metric{levels.gapfill.max_year_departure.year}). In \metric{levels.gapfill.check5.year}, the year with the most gap-filled scenes (\metric{levels.gapfill.check5.n_filled_scenes}), DAHITI minus shifted Hydroweb has a median of \metric{levels.gapfill.check5.median_resid_m}~m at the \metric{levels.gapfill.check5.n_pairs} DAHITI epochs of that year with near-simultaneous Hydroweb records, against a common 1$\sigma$ of \metric{levels.gapfill.check5.offset_sigma_p50_m}~m. These pairs also enter the shift, so this check is not independent.

\emph{Levels between records.} Between records, the level is modelled as a Brownian bridge with measurement error. For an acquisition at fraction $u$ of a gap of $\Delta t$ days between records $i$ and $j$,
\begin{equation}
  h = (1-u)\,h_i + u\,h_j, \qquad
  \sigma_h^2 = (1-u)^2\sigma_i^2 + u^2\sigma_j^2 + q\,\Delta t\,u(1-u),
\end{equation}
with $q = \metric{levels.q_m2_per_day}$~m\textsuperscript{2}\,d\textsuperscript{$-1$} (standard error \metric{levels.se.q_m2_per_day}~m\textsuperscript{2}\,d\textsuperscript{$-1$}), estimated jointly with $\sigma$ as described above. $q$ describes the error of linear interpolation in this series, including variability between records such as the curvature of the seasonal cycle, not the sea alone. The same $q$ is used for Hydroweb. Hydroweb's own fit gives \metric{levels.diag.hydroweb_free_q_m2_per_day}~m\textsuperscript{2}\,d\textsuperscript{$-1$}, and using it would change the 1$\sigma$ of gap-filled scenes by at most \metric{levels.diag.hydroweb_free_q_sigma_change_max_m}~m. For gap-filled scenes, the common error of the shift is added to $\sigma_h^2$ after interpolation.

\emph{Coverage of the scene record.} Levels were assigned to \metric{levels.n_scenes_by_source.dahiti} scenes from DAHITI and \metric{levels.n_scenes_by_source.hydroweb_shifted} from Hydroweb; the median 1$\sigma$ is \metric{levels.scene_sigma_p50_m}~m (99th percentile \metric{levels.scene_sigma_p99_m}~m). The \metric{levels.n_scenes_by_epoch.pre_altimetry} Landsat scenes acquired before the altimetry record, and the \metric{levels.n_scenes_by_epoch.no_level} scenes acquired after the last Hydroweb record, have no level, so they do not enter the level-binned topography.

\emph{Epochs.} The rising limb (epoch~E1) ends at the maximum of the \metric{levels.dahiti.smoothed.window_records}-record running median of the DAHITI series, on \metric{levels.epoch_E1_end}, so that a single noisy record cannot set it. The top of the running median is flat, so its date is uncertain: its two highest values differ by \metric{levels.diag.e1_end.top_two_margin_m}~m. With the single-record maximum, E1 would end on \metric{levels.diag.e1_end.k1.date} and contain \metric{levels.diag.e1_end.k1.n_E1} scenes. Epoch~E2 runs from then to \metric{params.e2_end}, and epoch~E3 after that. E1 contains \metric{levels.n_scenes_by_epoch.E1} scenes, E2 \metric{levels.n_scenes_by_epoch.E2} and E3 \metric{levels.n_scenes_by_epoch.E3}.

\subsection*{Bathymetry products audited}
We audited the global relief grids behind published Caspian studies and the current ones:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{GEBCO\_2022}\citep{gebco2022}, the basis of the exposure assessment of ref.~\citenum{court2025};
  \item \textbf{ETOPO1}\citep{amante2009}, the depth map of the vulnerability study of ref.~\citenum{akbari2020};
  \item \textbf{GEBCO\_2026}\citep{gebco2026};
  \item \textbf{ETOPO~2022}\citep{etopo2022,macferrin2025};
  \item \textbf{SRTM15+ V2.7}\citep{tozer2019}.
\end{itemize}
The grids are not independent. ETOPO~2022 takes GEBCO~2022 as its base layer for seas and large lakes\citep{macferrin2025}, and in the GEBCO Type Identifier grid the North Caspian sea area is coded as a pre-generated grid of mixed sources\citep{gebco2026}. GEBCO describes its source data as assimilated assuming a mean-sea-level reference, but notes that in some shallow-water areas the grid includes sources with other vertical datums\citep{gebco2026}. ETOPO1 is documented as referenced to sea level\citep{amante2009}. Because the effective vertical reference of each grid within the Caspian is not documented, every hindcast error is reported twice: as the total error, and as the shape error left after removing the single constant offset that best aligns the product with the observed extents.
