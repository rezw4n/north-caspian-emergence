A shrinking closed sea records its own floor, and the record can audit the maps used to project what it will expose next. On the bare, validated western and interfluve coasts of the North Caspian, \metric{rev.s.item1.shape.validated4.n_products_all} of five global bathymetry grids misplace three decades of observed shorelines by kilometres under every check, and on the northeastern coast for most, while an area-fitted shift matches their median areas; the fifth comes close only where it holds land heights acquired after the seabed emerged and a lake outline of 2000. The water-level pathway sets how much seabed the coming decades expose; the grids set where. Projections should measure from the observed shoreline, report the spread across grids, and seek new seabed data in the band that will emerge next.
