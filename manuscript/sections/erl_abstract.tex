\begin{abstract}
\noindent As closed seas and saline lakes shrink, projections of how much seabed will emerge, and of what it will expose, are built on global bathymetry grids. On a flat shelf a small vertical error in such a grid moves the predicted shoreline by kilometres, yet the grids are rarely tested against seabed that has already emerged. The North Caspian Sea, whose level fell by \metric{levels.dahiti.smoothed.range_m}~m between \metric{levels.dahiti.smoothed.date_of_max} and \metric{levels.dahiti.smoothed.date_of_min}, allows that test. We paired Landsat waterlines from 1992 to 2026 with satellite altimetry levels and asked whether five global grids, flooded from the open sea at each observed level, reproduce the observed shorelines. Of the five, \metric{erl.text.h1.n_products_majority} misplace them in \metric{erl.text.h1.majority.n_sectors_min} or \metric{erl.text.h1.majority.n_sectors_max} of the seven coastal sectors, by sector medians of \metric{erl.text.h1.majority.disp_min_km}--\metric{erl.text.h1.majority.disp_max_km}~km against observation noise floors of \metric{erl.text.h1.noise_p95_min_km}--\metric{erl.text.h1.noise_p95_max_km}~km, even after removing the vertical offset that best fits each grid. Yet their median flooded areas are within \metric{erl.text.h1.area_bias_shape_absmax_pct}\% of the observed, so whole-sea comparisons hide the error. The fifth grid owes its apparent skill largely to land heights acquired after the seabed had emerged; below the shorelines of that time it also errs by kilometres. The pattern recurs at Great Salt Lake, Utah, where all five grids fail and an elevation model built mostly from lidar passes the same test. For the first metre of further fall, which \metric{erl.text.h3.paths.1m.n_reached} of \metric{erl.text.h3.paths.n_primary} published pathways reach between \metric{erl.text.h3.paths.1m.first_year} and \metric{erl.text.h3.paths.1m.last_year}, the grids place \metric{erl.text.h3.uncorrected.1.min_km2}~\kmsq{} (\metric{erl.text.conformal_pct}\% interval \metric{erl.text.h3.uncorrected.1.min_lo_km2}--\metric{erl.text.h3.uncorrected.1.min_hi_km2}) to \metric{erl.text.h3.uncorrected.1.max_km2}~\kmsq{} (\metric{erl.text.h3.uncorrected.1.max_lo_km2}--\metric{erl.text.h3.uncorrected.1.max_hi_km2}) of seabed in the emerging band, and all five agree on only \metric{erl.agree.1m.all_pct_of_any}\% of the area that any of them includes. A pre-registered correction learned from the hindcast errors did not transfer to the lowest observed levels. Near-term seabed emergence in shrinking closed seas is limited by bathymetry, not only by the water-level pathway.
\end{abstract}
