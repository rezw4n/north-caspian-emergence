Projections of seabed emergence in a shrinking closed sea have two uncertain inputs, the fall of the water and the shape of the floor it uncovers. For the next metre or two of fall in the North Caspian, the second matters as much as the first. The primary pathways differ by decades in when the first metre of fall arrives; the grids differ by a factor of \metric{erl.text.h3.uncorrected.1.ratio} in how much seabed that metre exposes, and all five agree on where for only \metric{erl.agree.1m.all_pct_of_any}\% of it. An assessment built on one grid\citep{court2025,duan2025} inherits an error that a whole-sea check cannot reveal\citep{bocharov2026}, because the error lies in where the shoreline goes, not in the total area. That error matters most near the present shoreline, for ports and channels and for the protected deltaic wetlands of the Volga and Ural. The spread across grids is not an interval that contains the true emergence: every grid misplaces the historic shorelines below the land-data epoch, and the grids share sources.

Better estimates need seabed data in the zone that will emerge next, from surveys, charts or lidar bathymetry; in our search, ICESat-2's nearshore bathymetry product\citep{atl24_v2} held no data over the Caspian (Supplementary section~\SImethods). The waterline record offers a partial remedy: each metre of fall is surveyed by the next waterlines, so the audit can be repeated as the sea falls and grid heights replaced by observed ones.

The observations are Landsat only, at \metric{params.stage2_res_m}~m: Sentinel-2 was left out of the main run to stay within the computing budget, and the pre-registered rule for screening scenes by modelled wind setup selected no screening because the waterline scatter it measures stayed at one grid cell for every threshold. Independent noise floors, the falling-limb audit and the passing control argue against observation error, and per-sector datum shifts do not remove the grid errors (Supplementary section~\SIredteam). The Komsomolets Bay level departs from the basin level, so errors there may partly be an artefact of the level pairing. The deltaic and vegetated half of the audit hypothesis should be treated as unresolved. Alternative thresholds, sensors and season rules were tested on the elevation model, not the audit, and a few registered variants remain unrun (Supplementary section~\SIrobust).

Our waterline elevation model failed its pre-registered test, so the audit uses observed shorelines only, as pre-registered. On \metric{h2.atl08.n_test_blocks} held-out blocks of \metric{h2.block_size_km}~km, its NMAD against ICESat-2 land heights is \metric{h2.atl08.dem.nmad_m}~m, against \metric{erl.text.h2.atl08.product_nmad_min_m}--\metric{erl.text.h2.atl08.product_nmad_max_m}~m for the grids, but its root-mean-square error (RMSE; \metric{h2.atl08.dem.rmse_m}~m) is not lower than theirs (\metric{erl.text.h2.atl08.product_rmse_min_m}--\metric{erl.text.h2.atl08.product_rmse_max_m}~m), and the block-bootstrap \metric{params.ci_pct}\% intervals of the paired differences include zero for \metric{erl.text.h2.atl08.diff_nmad.n_ci_include_zero} (NMAD) and \metric{erl.text.h2.atl08.diff_rmse.n_ci_include_zero} (RMSE) of \metric{erl.text.h1.n_products} grids. It fails at the Volga delta front, probably where flooded reeds are classified as water, and in Komsomolets Bay. The verdict depends on which blocks are held out: it would pass against the ICESat-2 land and vegetation heights with \metric{params.block_size_sens_lo_km}~km blocks, and in \metric{robustness.check08.split_reassignment.atl08.n_seeds_h2_matched_supported} of \metric{robustness.check08.split_reassignment.n_seeds} random reassignments of the \metric{h2.block_size_km}~km blocks; the registered draw has a higher NMAD than \metric{erl.text.h2.split.atl08.n_seeds_lower_nmad} of them and lower coverage than \metric{erl.text.h2.split.atl08.n_seeds_higher_coverage}. The registered verdict stands. Its nominal \metric{erl.text.conformal_pct}\% intervals cover \metric{erl.text.pct.h2.atl08.coverage.overall}\% of test heights (block-bootstrap \metric{params.ci_pct}\% interval \metric{erl.text.pct.h2.atl08.coverage.overall_boot_low}--\metric{erl.text.pct.h2.atl08.coverage.overall_boot_high}\%), and the GLO-30 and GEDI checks give the same picture (Supplementary section~\SIdem).

The protected-area figures inherit the database's geometry. The Kazakh State Reserved Zone in the northern Caspian, which dominates the layer, is drawn an order of magnitude larger than its reported area and was used as published; no estimate without it has been made. The Ural Delta Ramsar site is recorded only as a point, so its exposure is not estimated, and Caspian seal habitat is not included.

The mechanism is not specific to the Caspian. Global grids are least constrained where soundings are sparse and vertical references undocumented\citep{weatherall2015,gebco2026}, as in the shallow North Caspian, and grids that fill recently emerged land from post-emergence elevation data will look skilful above the recent shoreline but not below it. Great Salt Lake reproduces both effects. The generality test covers one further basin, however: Lake Urmia was not run within the computing budget, and the control at Great Salt Lake is a post-emergence elevation model, mostly 2016 lidar\citep{quantumspatial2017_gsl,usgs_3dep13_tiles}, that shares the observed shorelines with the audit.
