\subsection{Observed shorelines}
The study domain is the North Caspian north of the line from Chechen Island to Cape Tyub-Karagan (\metric{study_area.domain_km2}~\kmsq{}, land included). Before any evaluation it was divided into seven sectors: the Kizlyar Bay--Dagestan--Kalmyk coast (west), the Volga delta, the Volga--Ural interfluve, the Ural delta, the northeastern Kazakh coast, Komsomolets Bay with the Kaidak flats, and the open shelf. Areas are computed in a Lambert azimuthal equal-area projection.

We used the Landsat~5, 7, 8 and 9 Collection~2 Level-2 scenes over the domain (\metric{erl.text.inventory.n_landsat} scenes)\citep{usgs_l45tm_c2l2,usgs_l7etm_c2l2,usgs_l89oli_c2l2}, processed in Google Earth Engine\citep{gorelick2017}. Pixels flagged as cloud, shadow or saturated were masked, and the months from \metric{params.ice_first_month} to \metric{params.ice_last_month}, when ice is common, were excluded. Each pixel was classified as water or dry with the modified normalised difference water index (MNDWI)\citep{xu2006} and a threshold set once per acquisition from its histogram. Pixels below the threshold with a normalised difference vegetation index above \metric{params.ndvi_veg} are emergent reeds, which hide the water's edge, and were treated as unobserved.

Each scene was given the basin level at its acquisition time, interpolated with its uncertainty from the altimetry series of the Database for Hydrological Time Series of Inland Waters (DAHITI)\citep{schwatke2015}: \metric{levels.dahiti.n_records} records from \metric{levels.dahiti.first} to \metric{levels.dahiti.last}, with long gaps filled from Hydroweb\citep{hydrowebwl,cretaux2011}. The median 1$\sigma$ of a scene level is \metric{levels.scene_sigma_p50_m}~m. All heights are compared on the DAHITI reference; a basin level converts to the Baltic Height System by adding \metric{datum.bhs.delta_m}~m ($\sigma = \metric{datum.bhs.sigma_posts_years_m}$~m), estimated from the official gauge levels of \metric{datum.bhs.n_posts} posts over \metric{datum.bhs.n_years} years\citep{caspcom2026}.

Scenes were grouped into level bins \metric{params.bin_width_m}~m wide, separately for three epochs: the rising limb (E1, to \metric{levels.epoch_E1_end}), the early fall (E2, to \metric{params.e2_end}) and the late fall (E3). For each epoch and bin, Earth Engine counted per \metric{params.stage2_res_m}~m cell the acquisitions in which the cell was water and those in which it was observed at all. A cell is observed sea at that level if it was water in at least \metric{params.contour_level} of at least \metric{params.min_valid_obs} valid acquisitions and is connected to the open sea. Because a cell under water at the record low is under water at every higher level, sea cells of the record low were kept as sea at every level; this removes deep, clear water that the index misreads as land. Alternate acquisitions of each bin form two independent halves, whose shorelines give the observation noise floor. A pre-registered rule for screening scenes by modelled wind setup selected \metric{screening.chosen_tau} (Supplementary section~\SImethods).

\subsection{Hindcast audit}
We audited five global grids: GEBCO\_2026\citep{gebco2026}, GEBCO\_2022\citep{gebco2022}, ETOPO 2022\citep{etopo2022,macferrin2025}, ETOPO1\citep{amante2009} and SRTM15+ V2.7\citep{tozer2019}. GEBCO\_2022 and ETOPO1 underlie published Caspian exposure and vulnerability assessments\citep{court2025,akbari2020}. The grids share sources (ETOPO 2022 takes GEBCO\_2022 as its base for seas and large lakes\citep{macferrin2025}), so they are partly dependent, not independent replications. At each observed level, each grid's predicted sea is every cell below the level that connects to the open sea through such cells. The grids' vertical reference in the Caspian is uncertain\citep{gebco2026,tozer2019}, so each grid was first shifted by the single constant that minimises its summed area error over all levels on training blocks (the shape variant, which decides); the grid on the documented datum chain, without a constant, is the total variant. Blocks of \metric{params.block_size_km}~km were assigned at random to training, calibration and test in proportions \metric{params.split_train}, \metric{params.split_calib} and \metric{params.split_test}, stratified by sector (seed \metric{params.seed}).

The shoreline displacement of a grid at a level is the median distance between its predicted shoreline cells and the observed ones, pooled in both directions. A grid mispredicts a sector if its displacement exceeds the \metric{params.err_pct_hi}th percentile of the noise floor at more than half of the sector's evaluable levels. Whether the errors are largest in the deltaic and vegetated sectors (west, Volga, Ural) was tested with a one-sided permutation test of block medians (\metric{params.permutations_n} permutations, $\alpha = \metric{params.alpha}$).

\subsection{Generality}
At Great Salt Lake (Utah, USA) the same lighter-weight pipeline audited the north and south arms, with levels from the gauges of the United States Geological Survey (USGS) on the North American Vertical Datum of 1988\citep{usgs_nwis}. A positive control, fixed in advance, was the 2016 lidar elevation model of the USGS 3D Elevation Program\citep{usgs_3dep10m}\todo{check: the cited catalogue page documents the 1/3 arc-second 3DEP DEM, not that its Great Salt Lake tiles come from 2016 lidar (D-094); source that detail or drop it}, evaluated only above its flattened lake surfaces.

\subsection{Near-term emergence}
The 2026 reference level is \metric{h3.level_2026_m}~m on the DAHITI reference (\metric{erl.text.h3.level_2026_bhs_m}~m in the Baltic Height System), the mean of the \metric{h3.level_2026.n_records} altimetry records of 2026. For declines of \metric{h3.scenarios_text}~m below it, a grid's emerged area is the area its flood fill covers at the 2026 level but not at the lower one, so each grid is referenced to its own 2026 shoreline. Split-conformal 90\% intervals\citep{lei2018} were calibrated on the absolute area errors of the hindcast at held-out observed levels (\metric{h3.n_scores.gebco2026.uncorrected} per grid). As pre-registered, each grid was also corrected by an error model learned from the hindcast (per sector, a Theil--Sen line of the matching vertical shift against depth, held constant beyond its fitted range; a constant shift was a registered sensitivity), with the waterline elevation model used above the record low. The correction's transfer was tested by leave-one-epoch-out, and it counted as decision-relevant at a decline if either estimate lay outside the other's 90\% interval. Exposure counts use OpenStreetMap features\citep{osm} and the herbaceous wetland class of ESA WorldCover 2021\citep{worldcover2021}. The dates at which published pathways reach each decline come from \citet{samant2023} and \citet{hoseini2025}, linear between the years each source states.

\subsection{Waterline elevation model and independent heights}
We also interpolated the E2 and E3 waterlines into a \metric{params.dem_res_m}~m elevation model of the emerged band and tested it, as pre-registered, against heights of the Ice, Cloud and land Elevation Satellite-2 (ICESat-2) over seabed already dry when measured\citep{atl06_v7,atl08_v7,markus2017}, on held-out blocks of \metric{h2.block_size_km}~km; heights from the Copernicus 30~m digital elevation model (GLO-30)\citep{copdem,copdem_handbook_i5} and the Global Ecosystem Dynamics Investigation (GEDI)\citep{gedi_l2a_v2} were further checks (Supplementary section~\SIdem).

\subsection{Pre-registration and reproducibility}
The hypotheses, metrics, blocks and decision rules were registered before any hindcast, topography-accuracy or projection metric was computed; dated amendments and deviations are listed in Supplementary section~\SIdeviations. Every number in this Letter is written by script from one results file.
