\subsection{Observed shorelines}
The study domain is the North Caspian north of the line from Chechen Island to Cape Tyub-Karagan (\metric{study_area.domain_km2}~\kmsq{}, land included), divided before any evaluation into seven sectors: the Kizlyar Bay--Dagestan--Kalmyk coast (west), the Volga and Ural deltas, the Volga--Ural interfluve, the northeastern Kazakh coast, Komsomolets Bay and the open shelf.

We used the Landsat~5, 7, 8 and 9 Collection~2 Level-2 scenes over the domain (\metric{erl.text.inventory.n_landsat} scenes)\citep{usgs_l45tm_c2l2,usgs_l7etm_c2l2,usgs_l89oli_c2l2}, processed in Google Earth Engine\citep{gorelick2017}. Clouds, shadows, saturated pixels and the ice months (\metric{params.ice_first_month} to \metric{params.ice_last_month}) were masked. Each pixel was classified as water or dry with the modified normalised difference water index (MNDWI)\citep{xu2006} and a threshold set once per acquisition from its histogram; pixels with a normalised difference vegetation index above \metric{params.ndvi_veg} are emergent reeds, which hide the water's edge, and were treated as unobserved.

Each scene was given the basin level at its acquisition time, interpolated with its uncertainty from the altimetry series of the Database for Hydrological Time Series of Inland Waters (DAHITI)\citep{schwatke2015}: \metric{levels.dahiti.n_records} records from \metric{levels.dahiti.first} to \metric{levels.dahiti.last}, with long gaps filled from Hydroweb\citep{hydrowebwl,cretaux2011}. The median 1$\sigma$ of a scene level is \metric{levels.scene_sigma_p50_m}~m. Heights are on the DAHITI reference; a level converts to the Baltic Height System by adding \metric{datum.bhs.delta_m}~m ($\sigma = \metric{datum.bhs.sigma_posts_years_m}$~m), estimated from the official gauge levels of \metric{datum.bhs.n_posts} posts over \metric{datum.bhs.n_years} years\citep{caspcom2026}.

Scenes were grouped into level bins \metric{params.bin_width_m}~m wide, separately for the rising limb (E1, to \metric{levels.epoch_E1_end}), the early fall (E2, to \metric{params.e2_end}) and the late fall (E3). Per \metric{params.stage2_res_m}~m cell, a level's observed sea consists of the cells that were water in at least \metric{params.contour_level} of at least \metric{params.min_valid_obs} valid acquisitions and connect to the open sea. Cells that are sea at the record low were kept as sea at every higher level, which removes deep, clear water misread as land. Alternate acquisitions of each bin form two independent halves, whose shorelines give the observation noise floor.

\subsection{Hindcast audit}
We audited five global grids: GEBCO\_2026\citep{gebco2026}, GEBCO\_2022\citep{gebco2022}, ETOPO 2022\citep{etopo2022,macferrin2025}, ETOPO1\citep{amante2009} and SRTM15+ V2.7\citep{tozer2019}. They share sources, so they are partly dependent\citep{macferrin2025,gebco2022}. With $z_j$ a grid's height of cell $j$ on the DAHITI reference and $S$ the open-sea seed cells, the flood level of cell $i$ is
% src/caspian/hindcast_tiled.py: flood_level_map (minimax path height, 8-connected, finite cells);
% src/caspian/products.py: flood_level (threshold L + c); scripts/a7_hindcast.py: fit_offset (c)
\begin{equation}
  F_i = \min_{\pi \in \Pi(S, i)} \; \max_{j \in \pi} z_j ,
  \label{eq:floodlevel}
\end{equation}
where $\Pi(S, i)$ is the set of 8-connected paths of cells with finite heights from $S$ to $i$; cell $i$ is predicted sea at level $L$ when $F_i < L + c$. The grids' vertical reference in the Caspian is uncertain\citep{gebco2026,tozer2019}, so $c$ is the single constant per grid that minimises its summed absolute area error over all observed levels on training blocks (the shape variant, which decides); $c = 0$ on the documented datum chain is the total variant. Blocks of \metric{params.block_size_km}~km were assigned at random to training, calibration and test in proportions \metric{params.split_train}, \metric{params.split_calib} and \metric{params.split_test}, stratified by sector (seed \metric{params.seed}).

The displacement $D_{s,L}$ of a grid in sector $s$ at level $L$ is the median distance between its predicted shoreline cells and the observed ones, pooled in both directions and over epochs. With $Q_{s,L}$ the \metric{params.err_pct_hi}th percentile of the same distances between the two observation halves, the grid mispredicts sector $s$ if
% src/caspian/h1.py: mispredicts (exceeds = median > p95_noise; mispredicts = n_exceeding > n_evaluable / 2)
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{L \in \mathcal{L}_s} \mathbf{1}\!\left[ D_{s,L} > Q_{s,L} \right] > \tfrac{1}{2}\,\lvert \mathcal{L}_s \rvert ,
  \label{eq:mispredicts}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_s$ is the set of evaluable levels of the sector (with an observed shoreline, a grid distance and a noise distance). Larger errors in the deltaic and vegetated sectors (west, Volga, Ural) were tested with a one-sided permutation test of block medians (\metric{params.permutations_n} permutations, $\alpha = \metric{params.alpha}$).

\subsection{Generality}
At Great Salt Lake (Utah, USA) the same pipeline audited the north and south arms, with levels from the gauges of the United States Geological Survey (USGS), recorded on the National Geodetic Vertical Datum of 1929\citep{usgs_nwis} and converted by us to the North American Vertical Datum of 1988 with VERTCON~3.0\citep{noaa_ncat}. A positive control, fixed in advance, was the elevation model of the USGS 3D Elevation Program\citep{usgs_3dep10m}, built over the lake bed mostly from lidar of autumn 2016\citep{quantumspatial2017_gsl,usgs_3dep13_tiles} and hydro-flattened\citep{archuleta2017}, evaluated only above its flattened lake surfaces.

\subsection{Near-term emergence}
The 2026 reference level $L_{2026}$ is \metric{h3.level_2026_m}~m on the DAHITI reference (\metric{erl.text.h3.level_2026_bhs_m}~m in the Baltic Height System), the mean of the \metric{h3.level_2026.n_records} altimetry records of 2026. For a decline $\Delta$ of \metric{h3.scenarios_text}~m, a grid's emerged area is
% src/caspian/exposure.py: emerged_curve; scripts/a8_emergence.py (sea cells F < L in the domain)
\begin{equation}
  E(\Delta) = A(L_{2026}) - A(L_{2026} - \Delta) ,
  \label{eq:emerged}
\end{equation}
where $A(L)$ is the area of the domain cells with $F_i < L$, so each grid is referenced to its own 2026 shoreline. Split-conformal \metric{erl.text.conformal_pct}\% intervals\citep{lei2018} were calibrated on the absolute errors of the hindcast emerged area at held-out observed levels (\metric{h3.n_scores.gebco2026.uncorrected} per grid). As pre-registered, each grid was also corrected by an error model learned from the hindcast, its transfer tested by leaving out one epoch, and the correction judged decision-relevant at a decline if either estimate lay outside the other's \metric{erl.text.conformal_pct}\% interval (Supplementary section~\SImethods). Exposure counts use OpenStreetMap features\citep{osm}, the herbaceous wetland class of ESA WorldCover 2021\citep{worldcover2021}, and the protected areas and Ramsar sites of the October 2026 World Database on Protected and Conserved Areas (www.protectedplanet.net)\citep{wdpa}. The dates at which published pathways reach each decline come from \citet{samant2023} and \citet{hoseini2025}, linear between the years each source states.

\subsection{Waterline elevation model and independent heights}
We also interpolated the E2 and E3 waterlines into a \metric{params.dem_res_m}~m elevation model of the emerged band and tested it, as pre-registered, against heights of the Ice, Cloud and land Elevation Satellite-2 (ICESat-2)\citep{atl06_v7,atl08_v7,markus2017} over seabed already dry when measured, on held-out blocks of \metric{h2.block_size_km}~km, with the Copernicus 30~m elevation model (GLO-30)\citep{copdem,copdem_handbook_i5} and the Global Ecosystem Dynamics Investigation (GEDI)\citep{gedi_l2a_v2} as further checks (Supplementary section~\SIdem).

\subsection{Pre-registration and reproducibility}
The hypotheses, metrics, blocks and decision rules were registered before any hindcast, topography-accuracy or projection metric was computed; dated amendments and deviations are listed in Supplementary section~\SIdeviations. Every number in this Letter is written by script from one results file. The analysis code, tests and figure scripts were written and run by an AI agent based on a large language model (Claude Code, Anthropic) under the authors' direction. Each analytical choice was logged with its reason before results were seen, or labelled as made afterwards, in the repository's decision log, and the authors approved them (see Use of generative AI).
