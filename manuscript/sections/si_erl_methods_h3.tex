\subsection*{Near-term emergence and exposure}
\emph{Reference level and scenarios.} The 2026 reference level is the mean of the \metric{h3.level_2026.n_records} DAHITI records of 2026, \metric{h3.level_2026_m}~m on the DAHITI reference (\metric{erl.text.h3.level_2026_bhs_m}~m in BHS-77); the latest three-record running median is a sensitivity. The scenarios are declines of \metric{h3.scenarios_text}~m below it; the pre-registered decision rule uses the \metric{h3.n_near_term} near-term declines of 0.5, 1 and 2~m.

\emph{Surfaces.} The \emph{uncorrected} surface is each grid as the hindcast reads it on the DAHITI reference through the per-cell datum chain (the total variant), with no constant and no error model: the grid as users apply it. The \emph{corrected} surface applies the registered error model. Its input is, per grid, observed unit $u$ (epoch and level bin, level $L_u$) and sector, the matching shift $\delta_u$: the constant of the shape-constant grid $\mathcal{C}$ (equation~\ref{eq:si_shape}) that minimises the absolute total area bias of the unit in the sector, with the same tie rules. It is usable when the bias changes sign on $\mathcal{C}$ and the sector holds both observed water and observed land at the unit. With $L_{\min}$ the lowest fitted level, the depth of the matching contour below it, the error model and the corrected height are
% src/caspian/exposure.py: matching_shift, unit_depths (d_u = L_min - L_u - delta_u), fit_model (Theil-Sen,
% scipy.stats.theilslopes; guards MIN_MONOTONE and MIN_UNITS), predict_shift (clip for linear_hold),
% fit_models (E2 + E3; domain model below MIN_UNITS), CorrectedSurface (z' = z - delta(L_min - z))
\begin{equation}
  d_u = L_{\min} - L_u - \delta_u , \qquad
  \delta(d) = a + b \, \operatorname{clip}\!\left(d;\, d_{\mathrm{lo}}, d_{\mathrm{hi}}\right) , \qquad
  z' = z - \delta\!\left(L_{\min} - z\right) ,
  \label{eq:si_errormodel}
\end{equation}
where $(a, b)$ is the Theil--Sen line\citep{sen1968} through the usable $(d_u, \delta_u)$ of E2 and E3 in the sector, $[d_{\mathrm{lo}}, d_{\mathrm{hi}}]$ the range of the fitted $d_u$, and $z$ the grid height on the DAHITI reference. This registered form, \texttt{linear\_hold}, holds the shift constant beyond the fitted depths. A fit with $1 + b < \metric{params.h3_min_monotone}$ falls back to the constant form, and a sector with fewer than \metric{params.h3_min_units} usable units uses the model of the whole domain. The registered sensitivities are the \texttt{constant} form ($a$ the median of the $\delta_u$, $b = 0$, so inside the fitted shifts by construction) and the \texttt{linear} form (no clip). The \emph{fused} surface is the waterline elevation model where it is finite and the cell is not known sea at the lowest observed unit, and the corrected grid elsewhere, flagged as extrapolation; where the grid contradicts the lowest observed extent it is clamped at $L_{\min}$, and such cells are counted.

\emph{Why the registered form degenerates.} The Theil--Sen slope is the median of the pairwise slopes $(\delta_u - \delta_v)/(d_u - d_v)$. Grids that store depths in whole metres put many units at nearly the same $d_u$, because the contour that matches the observed one sits at the same stored height, so many pairwise slopes are very steep, and so is $b$. The clip bounds $d$, not $\delta$: inside its own depth range the line runs from $a + b\,d_{\mathrm{lo}}$ to $a + b\,d_{\mathrm{hi}}$, which can lie far outside the range of the fitted $\delta_u$. The guard on $1 + b$, which keeps the corrected heights increasing with $z$, catches steep negative slopes but not steep positive ones. In \metric{h3.atl24.error_model.n_outside_fit_range} of \metric{h3.atl24.error_model.n_models} grid--sector models the shift applied in the near-term zone lies outside the fitted shifts (section~\SIredteam, R3).

\emph{Emerged area.} For a decline $\Delta$, $E(\Delta) = A(L_{2026}) - A(L_{2026} - \Delta)$ (equation~(\ref{M-eq:emerged}) of the Letter), where $A(L)$ is the area of the cells of the domain whose flood level on the surface is below $L$. Each surface is thus referenced to its own 2026 shoreline; the observed 2026 extent as a common baseline is a sensitivity.

\emph{Intervals.} Split-conformal intervals\citep{lei2018} are calibrated on hindcast errors of the emerged area. In the leave-one-epoch-out hindcast (fit on two epochs, predict the third), let $u^{\ast}$ be the held-out epoch's highest unit. For each other unit $u$ of that epoch at an observed level, over the cells $\mathcal{K}_{u^{\ast} u}$ known at both units, the score and the interval of a scenario estimate $\hat{E}$ are
% scripts/a8_emergence.py: leave-one-epoch-out errors ("emergence" functional: pred_top - pred_u against
% obs_top - obs_u over the cells known in both units; anchors and unobserved levels not scored);
% src/caspian/exposure.py: interval (q-hat of |e| by conformal.conformal_quantile; clipped to [0, A(L26)])
\begin{equation}
  e_u = \left(\hat{A}_{u^{\ast}} - \hat{A}_{u}\right) - \left(A^{\mathrm{obs}}_{u^{\ast}} - A^{\mathrm{obs}}_{u}\right) , \qquad
  \left[\, \max\!\left(0,\ \hat{E} - \hat{q}\right) ,\ \min\!\left(A(L_{2026}),\ \hat{E} + \hat{q}\right) \right] ,
  \label{eq:si_h3interval}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{A}$ is the predicted and $A^{\mathrm{obs}}$ the observed sea area over $\mathcal{K}_{u^{\ast} u}$, and $\hat{q}$ is the conformal quantile of equation~(\ref{eq:si_conformal}) applied to the $\lvert e_u \rvert$ at nominal coverage \metric{params.conformal_level} (\metric{h3.n_scores.gebco2026.uncorrected} scores per grid and surface; $\hat{q} = \infty$ when the scores are too few). Intervals are calibrated per grid, surface, region and quantity and never summed. The corrected and fused surfaces use the corrected grid's scores. The interval from the scores of the lowest held-out epoch alone is a sensitivity.

\emph{Transfer and decision rule.} For each held-out epoch, the transfer test compares the median absolute emergence error of the corrected and the uncorrected grid at that epoch's scored units, and reports the median error of the transferred shift. The correction is decision-relevant at a decline if the corrected estimate lies outside the \metric{erl.text.conformal_pct}\% interval of the uncorrected one, or the reverse. Per grid and for the domain, H3 is ``supported'' if the correction is decision-relevant at all three near-term declines and ``partial'' if at some; grids are never pooled, and exposure does not enter the rule.

\emph{Exposure.} From OpenStreetMap\citep{osm}: oil and gas platforms and wells newly onshore (\metric{h3.exposure.platforms.n_features} features in the domain); pipeline (\metric{h3.exposure.pipelines.n_features}) and channel (\metric{h3.exposure.channels.n_features}) length that emerges; ports, piers and ferry terminals (\metric{h3.exposure.ports.n_features}) that have the sea within \metric{h3.port_access_km}~km in 2026 but not at the scenario level; and settlements (\metric{h3.exposure.settlements.n_features}) with the sea within \metric{h3.coast_km}~km in 2026 but not at the scenario level. Habitat stand-ins with open licences: the herbaceous wetland class of ESA WorldCover 2021\citep{worldcover2021}, the swamp, marsh, flooded-flat and saline classes of GWL\_FCS30 2020\citep{zhang2023} and the wetland classes of the Global Lakes and Wetlands Database version~2 other than waterbodies and rice paddies\citep{lehner2025}. Exposure is counted at the scenario level and at the levels equivalent to the bounds of the area interval, rounded outward. Protected areas and Ramsar sites come from the public release of October 2026 of the World Database on Protected and Conserved Areas, downloaded for Kazakhstan and Russia, the only countries the domain reaches, and used under its terms for non-commercial research; only derived areas are reported. The provider requires this citation: UNEP-WCMC and IUCN (2026), Protected Planet: The World Database on Protected and Conserved Areas (WDPCA) [On-line], October 2026, Cambridge, UK: UNEP-WCMC and IUCN. Available at: \url{www.protectedplanet.net}\citep{wdpa}. The protected-area layer holds every polygon record of a protected area whose status is not ``Proposed'' or ``Not Reported'' (\metric{h3.exposure.wdpa.n_records_in_domain} records in the domain, \metric{h3.exposure.wdpa.n_mab_records_in_domain} of them UNESCO biosphere reserves, which the database classes as protected areas); the Ramsar layer holds the records designated as Wetlands of International Importance. Each layer is the union of its polygons, so overlapping designations count once, and its emerged area is counted with the area rule above. Its features are counted whole, including any part outside the domain, so the share of emergence inside protected areas is approximate. Point records (\metric{h3.exposure.wdpa.n_point_records_in_domain} in the domain) have no boundary and were not used. Three properties of this release shape the results, and none was corrected. The Kazakh ``State Reserved Zone in Northern part of Caspian Sea'' is drawn as a polygon about an order of magnitude larger than its reported area; it covers most of the eastern shelf and dominates the protected-area layer. It was used as published, and no estimate without it has been made. The Ural River Delta Ramsar site is recorded only as a point, so the Ramsar layer is the Volga Delta site alone and Ural-delta Ramsar exposure is not estimated. The Volga Delta polygon (\metric{h3.exposure.ramsar.area_in_domain_km2}~\kmsq{}) is larger than its reported area. Key Biodiversity Areas, Important Marine Mammal Areas and the range of the Caspian seal were not used: each needs a data request or registration with the owning organisation.

\emph{Agreement between grids.} For the agreement map of the Letter (figure~\ref{M-fig:erl_agreement}), with $F^{p}_i$ the flood level of cell $i$ on grid $p$ as users apply it, the number of grids that place cell $i$ in the band that emerges with a decline $\Delta$ is
% scripts/f1_erl_display.py: agreement (count of products with F < L26 and not F < L26 - D, domain cells
% in a sector; areas for counts >= 1, >= len // 2 + 1 and == len; checked against emergence.csv)
\begin{equation}
  N_i(\Delta) = \sum_{p=1}^{P} \mathbf{1}\!\left[ F^{p}_i < L_{2026} \ \wedge\ F^{p}_i \ge L_{2026} - \Delta \right] ,
  \label{eq:si_agreement}
\end{equation}
over the domain cells of the seven sectors, with $P$ the number of grids. The map reports the areas with $N_i \ge 1$ (any grid), $N_i \ge \lfloor P/2 \rfloor + 1$ (a majority) and $N_i = P$ (all grids); for each grid the cells it places in the band reproduce its emerged area of equation~(\ref{M-eq:emerged}) in the Letter.

\emph{Scenario dates.} The primary pathways are the best-fit SSP2-4.5 and SSP5-8.5 totals and the observed 1996--2021 trend of \citet{samant2023}, and the medians of the low, medium, medium-to-high and high emission scenarios of \citet{hoseini2025}. Each pathway's own change after its base year is applied from the 2026 level, linearly between the years its source states and never beyond 2100. The quotes behind every pathway were checked against the retrieved sources. Range and sensitivity pathways are tabulated beside them.

\emph{Independent seabed check (pre-registration Amendment~17).} ICESat-2 ATL24 nearshore bathymetry (version~2)\citep{atl24_v2,is2_atl24_atbd_v2} was to test the surfaces below the 2026 waterline. The \metric{h3.atl24.n_granules_found} granules that the NASA Common Metadata Repository lists for the domain's bounding box (\metric{h3.atl24.size_mb_cmr}~MB) were read in place: the data extent of \metric{h3.atl24.n_granules_extent_in_box} of them meets the box, all extents lying between \metric{h3.atl24.extent.lon_min_deg}\textdegree{} and \metric{h3.atl24.extent.lon_max_deg}\textdegree{}E and \metric{h3.atl24.extent.lat_min_deg}\textdegree{} and \metric{h3.atl24.extent.lat_max_deg}\textdegree{}N (the Persian Gulf), and \metric{h3.atl24.spot_check.n_with_box_photons} of \metric{h3.atl24.spot_check.n_sampled} granules drawn at random for a full read hold a photon in the box. Every verdict of the check is therefore ``\metric{h3.atl24.gebco2026.verdict}''. The documents read do not say why the Caspian is absent.
