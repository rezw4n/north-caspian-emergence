\subsection*{Water classification}
\emph{Active zone.} Scenes were classified only within an active zone, a band meant to contain the waterlines of the altimetry era. Its bounds are the water extents of the \metric{params.highstand_label} high stand and the \metric{params.lowstand_label} low stand, mapped from clear May--September observations. For these two extents only, a pixel counts as water if its MNDWI (below) is above zero, or if its near-infrared and first shortwave-infrared reflectances are both below \metric{params.dark_water_reflectance}: clear, deep water in the south of the domain is dark in both bands, and its MNDWI scatters around zero. The landward bound is the near-maximum extent of the high stand: pixels that were water in at least \metric{params.high_extent_min_water_pct}\% of clear Landsat~5 observations between \metric{params.highstand_start} and \metric{params.highstand_end}. Where fewer than \metric{params.min_obs_high} such observations exist, the maximum extent of the Global Surface Water record\citep{pekel2016} takes their place. The seaward bound is the near-minimum extent of the low stand: pixels that were water in at least \metric{params.low_extent_min_water_pct}\% of clear Landsat~8--9 observations between \metric{params.lowstand_start} and \metric{params.lowstand_end}. Only the part of the band between the two extents that is connected to the open sea is kept. The open sea is low-stand water whose surroundings within \metric{params.sea_radius_km}~km are at least \metric{params.sea_min_water_pct}\% water, which excludes lakes. High-stand water is kept if a path through high-stand water, at most \metric{params.conn_max_km}~km long, joins it to the open sea. The paths are traced on a \metric{params.conn_scale_km}~km grid on which a cell counts as water if any of its pixels does, so that narrow channels stay connected. The connected band was dilated by \metric{params.zone_dilate_m}~m on a \metric{params.zone_scale_m}~m grid in the equal-area projection. Per-pixel computation was limited to the \metric{params.zone_tile_km}~km cells that contain the zone. The low-stand bound reflects the levels of the May--September scenes; lower levels were reached in other months, so whether the zone contains the waterlines of the lowest level bins is checked for each level and sector before any hindcast comparison.

\emph{Snow and ice.} Two tests flag snow or ice in cloud-free pixels. The first is the product quality layer: the snow bit of the Landsat quality band\citep{usgs_l45tm_c2l2,usgs_l7etm_c2l2,usgs_l89oli_c2l2}, or Sentinel-2 scene-classification class \metric{params.s2_scl_snow}. The second is the conservative snow test of the Theia snow algorithm\citep{gascoin2019}: a normalised difference snow index above \metric{params.ndsi_snow} together with red reflectance above \metric{params.red_snow}. The red-reflectance condition keeps turbid water from being flagged as snow. Pixels flagged by either test were removed from \metric{params.snow_season_first_month} to \metric{params.snow_season_last_month}. A test was applied in every month only if, in each May--September month, it flagged at most \metric{params.snow_rule_max_pct}\% of the clear pixels in the zone (pre-registration Amendments~3 and~9). The share flagged by each test is recorded for every scene and month.

\emph{Ice season.} A calendar month was treated as ice-affected if, in the median scene of either sensor family, at least \metric{params.snow_rule_max_pct}\% of the clear pixels in the zone were flagged by either snow test, applied in every month for this purpose. By this rule the months from \metric{params.ice_first_month} to \metric{params.ice_last_month} were excluded from the water frequencies. The rule was set after the monthly flagged shares of the scene table had been inspected; a less strict bar, which keeps November, is a robustness variant.

\emph{Indices.} We computed the modified normalised difference water index (MNDWI) from the green and first shortwave-infrared bands\citep{xu2006}, and the normalised difference vegetation index (NDVI) from the near-infrared and red bands. The surface reflectance of clear water is often slightly negative in the near and shortwave infrared, and Earth Engine's normalised-difference operator masks pixels with a negative input\todo{cite: Earth Engine API documentation of ee.Image.normalizedDifference}. Negative reflectances were therefore set to zero before either index was formed, so that both indices lie between \metric{params.mndwi_min} and \metric{params.mndwi_max} and are undefined only where both inputs are zero. This corrected an earlier definition that had dropped clear-water observations before any water frequency existed, and every threshold was recomputed with it (pre-registration Amendment~11).

\emph{Threshold.} The water threshold was set once per acquisition (one overpass: a Landsat sensor, path and date, or a Sentinel-2 datatake) by Otsu's method\citep{otsu1979}, as the value that maximises the between-class variance of the MNDWI histogram. Each scene's histogram has \metric{params.otsu_bins} equal bins between \metric{params.mndwi_min} and \metric{params.mndwi_max} and is sampled at \metric{params.hist_scale_m}~m from the clear pixels inside the active zone; the histograms of the scenes of one acquisition were summed. Sampling inside the zone replaces the edge-guided sample of the pre-registered design, at far lower computing cost (pre-registration Amendment~3); the edge-guided threshold is computed for a random sample of \metric{params.edge_otsu_sample_n} scenes as a robustness check. A pooled threshold was accepted if all of the following held: the sample has at least \metric{params.thr_min_pixels} pixels; each class holds at least \metric{params.thr_min_class_pct}\% of them; the two-class split explains at least \metric{params.thr_min_eta} of the histogram's variance; the valley ratio, the lowest density between the two class peaks of the histogram smoothed over \metric{params.thr_smooth_bins} bins divided by the lower peak, is at most \metric{params.thr_max_valley}; and the threshold lies between \metric{params.thr_lo} and \metric{params.thr_hi}. The lower bound is physical. Open water absorbs more strongly in the shortwave infrared than in the green, so its MNDWI is positive; a negative threshold, found where the sample in the zone is mostly land, separates dry from moist land. Other acquisitions took the median of the accepted thresholds of the same sensor and calendar month. These constants were fixed from the histogram diagnostics, before any water frequency was computed. Of the \metric{thresholds.n_acquisitions} acquisitions of the open-water months, \metric{thresholds.valid_pct}\% had an accepted threshold. Each threshold is stored, so the per-pixel classification is a single comparison.

\emph{Classes.} Pixels above the threshold are water. Pixels below it with an NDVI above \metric{params.ndvi_veg} are emergent vegetation; these reed beds hide the water's edge, so they are treated as unobserved. All remaining pixels are dry. A pixel whose MNDWI is undefined, or that is below the threshold with an undefined NDVI, is unobserved. Overlapping scenes of one overpass (adjacent Landsat rows, or Sentinel-2 tiles of one datatake) were mosaicked first, so that each overpass counts at most once per pixel. The automated water extraction index AWEI\textsubscript{sh}\citep{feyisa2014} and a fixed MNDWI threshold are the alternatives tested in the robustness checks.
