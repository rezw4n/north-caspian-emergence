\subsection{Global grids misplace historic shorelines on the validated coasts}
Under the registered rule (equation~(\ref{eq:mispredicts})), \metric{erl.text.h1.n_products_majority} of the five grids (\metric{erl.text.h1.majority_products}) mispredict the historic shorelines in \metric{erl.text.h1.majority.n_sectors_min} or \metric{erl.text.h1.majority.n_sectors_max} of the seven sectors after an area-fitted vertical shift, and every grid does so in \metric{erl.text.h1.total.n_sectors_min} of the seven without it (figures~\ref{fig:erl_shorelines} and \ref{fig:erl_audit}; table~\ref{tab:reportcard}).

The evidence is firmest on the bare, validated coasts. On the west and the interfluve, the same \metric{rev.s.item1.shape.validated4.n_products_all} grids mispredict under every check: their sector medians of displacement, \metric{rev.w.h1.coasts3.d_km_min}--\metric{rev.w.h1.coasts3.d_km_max}~km against floors of \metric{rev.w.h1.coasts3.q_km_min}--\metric{rev.w.h1.coasts3.q_km_max}~km (median $D/Q$ \metric{rev.w.h1.coasts3.dq_min}--\metric{rev.w.h1.coasts3.dq_max}), far exceed those of a control made from the waterline model rounded to whole metres and aggregated to each grid's cells (\metric{rev.w.r.control.westinter_km_min}--\metric{rev.w.r.control.westinter_km_max}~km), and they survive a resolution-matched floor, a systematic error budget and the best shift for each sector. On the northeastern coast they hold for most grids and checks. The open shelf cannot be told apart from quantisation, resolution and level error: the grids miss by \metric{rev.s.headline.open_shelf.d_km_min}--\metric{rev.s.headline.open_shelf.d_km_max}~km against a floor of \metric{rev.s.headline.open_shelf.q_km}~km, close to the control's \metric{rev.w.r.control.openshelf_km_min}--\metric{rev.w.r.control.openshelf_km_max}~km, and the verdict turns on the record-low rule. In the Volga and Ural deltas and Komsomolets Bay, whose observations are not validated, the medians are \metric{rev.w.h1.unvalidated.d_km_min}--\metric{rev.w.h1.unvalidated.d_km_max}~km against floors of \metric{rev.w.h1.unvalidated.q_km_min}--\metric{rev.w.h1.unvalidated.q_km_max}~km, and grid and observation error cannot be separated.

Filtering static channel-bank waterlines changes no verdict; adding a systematic level budget (standard deviation \metric{rev.w.r.budget.sigma_median_min}--\metric{rev.w.r.budget.sigma_median_max}~m) removes the open shelf and, for two grids, the northeastern coast. Neither a displacement-fitted shift, \metric{rev.w.r.cD_below_min_m}--\metric{rev.w.r.cD_below_max_m}~m below the area-fitted one, nor levels shifted by each sector's measured water-surface departure changes a deciding verdict (Supplementary section~\SIrevision). Seabed change between epochs is not excluded: at the levels observed on both limbs the oldest seabed fits better in \metric{rev.s.item8.votes.k_e1_better} of \metric{rev.s.item8.votes.n} sector-level comparisons ($p = \metric{rev.s.item8.votes.p}$), and the two limbs' shorelines differ by \metric{rev.s.item8.hysteresis.northeast.median_km}--\metric{rev.s.item8.hysteresis.west.median_km}~km on the western and northeastern coasts.

\subsection{An area-fitted shift matches the median area, not the shoreline}
Because the shift is fitted to area, each grid's median area bias is within \metric{erl.text.h1.area_bias_shape_absmax_pct}\% after it (figure~\ref{fig:erl_audit}(a)), against \metric{erl.text.h1.area_bias_total_min_pct}--\metric{erl.text.h1.area_bias_total_max_pct}\% on the documented datum chain. Across levels the error remains: four grids put too little sea at the lowest levels and too much at the highest, by \metric{rev.w.s.slope.domain_min}--\metric{rev.w.s.slope.domain_max}\% per metre (Theil--Sen slopes with \metric{params.ci_pct}\% intervals above zero), and ETOPO 2022 shows no trend. Out of sample, a shift fitted on the two earlier epochs leaves the latest epoch with median area biases of \metric{rev.s.item4.oos.calibtest.c_e12.median_max_pct} to \metric{rev.s.item4.oos.calibtest.c_e12.median_min_pct}\% on held-out blocks. A whole-sea comparison that fitted no offset and tested no global grid\citep{bocharov2026} corresponds here to the datum chain. An area-fitted shift removes the median area bias in sample only, and agreement in total area is no evidence that a grid puts the emerging seabed in the right place.

The second half of the registered hypothesis, that errors are largest in the deltaic and vegetated sectors, is not supported. The registered block permutation test was significant for \metric{erl.text.h1.block.shape.significant_products}, but blocks within a sector are not exchangeable: at sector level their exact one-sided $p$ is \metric{rev.w.s.item5.sector_p_min}--\metric{rev.w.s.item5.sector_p_max} (with seven sectors it cannot fall below \metric{rev.s.item5.sector.min_attainable_p}), and after Holm's adjustment over the ten registered block tests none remains significant (\metric{rev.w.s.item5.block_p_holm_min}--\metric{rev.w.s.item5.block_p_holm_max}). This half is unresolved.

\subsection{ETOPO 2022's skill comes from FABDEM and its lake outline, not from lake depths}
ETOPO 2022 mispredicts \metric{h1.mispredicts.shape.etopo2022.n_sectors} sector after the shift (\metric{h1.mispredicts.total.etopo2022.n_sectors} without it) and no validated sector. Its Caspian lake depths are GEBCO\_2022's with every value above \metric{rev.w.g.etopo.cap_m}~m set to \metric{rev.w.g.etopo.cap_m}~m, inside a lake outline; neither ETOPO document states this cap, which matches the Caspian surface elevation of HydroLAKES, whose outlines show the shorelines of February 2000\citep{hydrolakes_doc,messager2016}. Landward of the outline it uses FABDEM, land heights from Copernicus radar data acquired after much of the seabed had emerged\citep{macferrin2025,hawker2022,copdem_handbook_i5}. Above \metric{h1.disp_band.edge_2_m}~m every ETOPO 2022 shoreline cell is therefore FABDEM or the outline's edge, never a depth contour, and its per-sector $D/Q$ is \metric{rev.w.g.etopo.above.dq_min}--\metric{rev.w.g.etopo.above.dq_max}. Below, it follows GEBCO\_2022 ($D/Q$ \metric{rev.w.g.etopo.below.dq_min}--\metric{rev.w.g.etopo.below.dq_max}), and every grid exceeds the floor at most levels in \metric{rev.w.g.etopo.below.n_sectors_min} or \metric{rev.w.g.etopo.below.n_sectors_max} of the seven sectors; on the open shelf ETOPO 2022 does so at \metric{rev.g.etopo.bands.below_m27p7.etopo2022.open_shelf.n_exceeding} of \metric{rev.g.etopo.bands.below_m27p7.etopo2022.open_shelf.n_evaluable} levels (figure~\ref{fig:erl_audit}(b)).

\subsection{At Great Salt Lake, grids without lake-bed data fail and a 3DEP surface passes}
No grid holds lake-bed soundings in either arm's emerged band: the GEBCO grids and SRTM15+ code every band cell as land or unsounded\citep{gebco2026,tozer2019} and, with ETOPO1, hold one flat height on \metric{rev.w.g.gsl.flat_share_pct_min}--\metric{rev.w.g.gsl.flat_share_pct_max}\% of it; ETOPO 2022's band is \metric{erl.text.gsl.north.etopo2022_fabdem_pct}\% FABDEM\citep{macferrin2025,copdem_handbook_i5}. Every grid mispredicts both arms, by \metric{erl.text.gsl.disp_min_m}--\metric{erl.text.gsl.disp_max_m}~m (figure~\ref{fig:erl_gsl}), also at the control's levels alone, where the grids' displacement is \metric{rev.w.g.gsl.ctrl.ratio_min}--\metric{rev.w.g.gsl.ctrl.ratio_max} times the control's. The 3DEP control passes, above the floor at \metric{generality.great_salt_lake.control.north.n_exceeding_levels} of \metric{generality.great_salt_lake.control.north.n_evaluable_levels} and \metric{generality.great_salt_lake.control.south.n_exceeding_levels} of \metric{generality.great_salt_lake.control.south.n_evaluable_levels} levels. ETOPO 2022 exceeds the floor at \metric{generality.great_salt_lake.etopo_2022.shape.north.n_exceeding_levels} of \metric{generality.great_salt_lake.etopo_2022.shape.north.n_evaluable_levels} north-arm levels, a majority, so it mispredicts that arm; with a floor pooled over levels it would not. Without the registered control, this test shows that the audit flags grids without lake-bed data and passes a post-emergence lidar surface, not that the Caspian errors recur.

\subsection{The pathway sets how much emerges; the grids set where}
As distributed, the grids differ by a factor of \metric{rev.n.summary.uncorrected.1.ratio} in the first metre of fall because whole-metre terraces fall either side of the 2026 level: raising that level by \metric{rev.w.n.sens.jitter_m}~m reverses the ranking of GEBCO\_2022 and ETOPO 2022 (\metric{rev.n.sens.L26+0.3.uncorrected.gebco2022.1.domain_km2} against \metric{rev.n.sens.L26+0.3.uncorrected.etopo2022.1.domain_km2}~\kmsq{}), and \metric{rev.w.n.dry.unc1_pct_min}--\metric{rev.w.n.dry.unc1_pct_max}\% of each band was already dry in 2025 and 2026. Measured from the observed 2026 shoreline, the grids place \metric{rev.n.composite.summary.common_location.1.min_km2}--\metric{rev.n.composite.summary.common_location.1.max_km2}~\kmsq{} of seabed in the first metre (hindcast envelopes \metric{rev.w.n.env.composite1.lo_min}--\metric{rev.w.n.env.composite1.hi_max}~\kmsq{}); shifted by their constants, \metric{rev.n.summary.shifted.1.min_km2}--\metric{rev.n.summary.shifted.1.max_km2}~\kmsq{} (\metric{rev.w.n.env.shifted1.lo_min}--\metric{rev.w.n.env.shifted1.hi_max}~\kmsq{}). Declines beyond \metric{rev.n.env.max_pair_decline_m}~m are uncalibrated, and the registered scale-aware rule gives no finite bound. Simulated vertical errors move the shifted grids' first metre by \metric{rev.w.n.sim.r50km.sd_rel_shifted1_pct_min}--\metric{rev.w.n.sim.r150km.sd_rel_shifted1_pct_max}\%, so they lie within each other's spread. Water cut off from the sea is at most \metric{rev.w.n.isolated.composite1_pct_max}\% of the band.

The low agreement on where is not evidence by itself: all five grids agree on \metric{rev.w.n.sim.agree_unc1_observed_pct}\% of the first metre that any places there, and five perturbed copies of one grid agree on \metric{rev.w.n.sim.r150km.agree_unc1_ref_p05_pct_min}--\metric{rev.w.n.sim.r50km.agree_unc1_ref_p95_pct_max}\% at correlation ranges of \metric{rev.w.n.sim.range_secondary_km} and \metric{rev.w.n.sim.range_primary_km}~km (figure~\ref{fig:erl_agreement}). How much emerges by a given year is set by the pathway, which carries \metric{rev.w.n.var.pathway_pct_min}--\metric{rev.w.n.var.pathway_pct_max}\% of the variance of emerged area across pathways and grids at 2035 and 2050; only from the observed shoreline in 2035 do the grids carry \metric{rev.w.n.var.composite2035.grid_area_pct}\% (figure~\ref{fig:erl_timeline}). Of the \metric{erl.text.h3.paths.n_primary} primary pathways, \metric{erl.text.h3.paths.1m.n_reached} reach the first metre between \metric{erl.text.h3.paths.1m.first_year} and \metric{erl.text.h3.paths.1m.last_year}, the trend of 2016--2025 by \metric{rev.w.n.trend.2016_2025.year_1m}. Exposure follows where each grid puts the shoreline and is a diagnostic of grid error: protected areas hold \metric{rev.w.n.wdpa.composite.with_pct_min}--\metric{rev.w.n.wdpa.composite.with_pct_max}\% of the first metre from the observed shoreline, but \metric{rev.w.n.wdpa.composite.without_pct_min}--\metric{rev.w.n.wdpa.composite.without_pct_max}\% without one polygon drawn an order of magnitude larger than the reserve it records (Supplementary section~\SInearterm).

The registered correction did not help: its error model applies shifts of up to \metric{h3.atl24.error_model.max_applied_shift_m}~m outside its fitted range, and fitted without the lowest epoch it is worse than no correction there for \metric{erl.text.h3.registered.e3_n_corrected_worse} of \metric{erl.text.h1.n_products} grids (verdict and details in Supplementary section~\SInearterm). The registered check below the 2026 waterline, ICESat-2 nearshore bathymetry (ATL24)\citep{atl24_v2}, was impossible: \metric{h3.atl24.n_granules_extent_in_box} of its \metric{h3.atl24.n_granules_found} listed granules cover the Caspian.
