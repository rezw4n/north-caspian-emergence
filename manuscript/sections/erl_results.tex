\subsection{Global grids misplace historic shorelines by kilometres}
Of the five grids, \metric{erl.text.h1.n_products_majority} (\metric{erl.text.h1.majority_products}) mispredict the historic shorelines in \metric{erl.text.h1.majority.n_sectors_min} or \metric{erl.text.h1.majority.n_sectors_max} of the seven sectors after the best-fitting vertical shift (figures~\ref{fig:erl_shorelines} and \ref{fig:erl_audit}; table~\ref{tab:reportcard}). Their sector medians of shoreline displacement range from \metric{erl.text.h1.majority.disp_min_km}~km to \metric{erl.text.h1.majority.disp_max_km}~km, against observation noise floors of \metric{erl.text.h1.noise_p95_min_km}--\metric{erl.text.h1.noise_p95_max_km}~km; the largest lie mostly in the Volga delta and Komsomolets Bay sectors (figure~\ref{fig:erl_shorelines}). In Komsomolets Bay the water level can depart from the basin level, which may add to the errors there. All five grids need nearly the same shift, \metric{erl.text.h1.shape_offset_min_m} to \metric{erl.text.h1.shape_offset_max_m}~m, so their vertical mismatch with the altimetric reference is shared. On the documented datum chain, without the shift, every grid mispredicts in \metric{erl.text.h1.total.n_sectors_min} of 7 sectors.

The errors do not come from how the shorelines were observed, nor from a datum that varies across the shelf (Supplementary sections~\SIrobust{} and \SIredteam). With a separate shift fitted for each sector, the same \metric{erl.text.h1.n_products_majority} grids still mispredict in \metric{erl.text.redteam.sector_shape.majority.n_sectors_min}--\metric{erl.text.redteam.sector_shape.majority.n_sectors_max} sectors; without the record-low rule, in \metric{erl.text.redteam.noenv.majority.n_sectors_min}--\metric{erl.text.redteam.noenv.majority.n_sectors_max}; and with the falling-limb epochs alone, in \metric{erl.text.robust.e2e3.majority.n_sectors_min}. Nor does seabed change between epochs explain the errors: at levels observed on both the rising and the falling limb, the grids fit the older seabed better at only \metric{erl.text.robust.morph_share_e1_better_pct}\% of levels.

\subsection{Totals look right while shorelines are wrong}
After the shift, the median area bias of each grid over the whole domain is within \metric{erl.text.h1.area_bias_shape_absmax_pct}\% of the observed sea (figure~\ref{fig:erl_audit}(a)), and the median intersection over union of predicted and observed sea is \metric{erl.text.h1.iou_shape_min}--\metric{erl.text.h1.iou_shape_max}. This matches the whole-sea comparison in which areas from a chart-based model were within \metric{lit.bocharov2026.max_area_diff_pct}\% of satellite areas\citep{bocharov2026}. The same grids nevertheless misplace the shoreline by kilometres sector by sector, so agreement in total area is no evidence that a grid puts the emerging seabed in the right place. At single levels the largest domain area bias is \metric{erl.text.h1.area_bias_shape_levelmax_min_pct}--\metric{erl.text.h1.area_bias_shape_levelmax_max_pct}\%, depending on the grid.

The second half of the pre-registered audit hypothesis, that the errors are largest in the deltaic and vegetated sectors, holds for \metric{erl.text.h1.block.shape.significant_products} (one-sided block permutation $p = \metric{erl.text.p4.h1.block_test.shape.gebco2026.p}$, \metric{erl.text.p4.h1.block_test.shape.gebco2022.p} and \metric{erl.text.p4.h1.block_test.shape.etopo1.p}), not for SRTM15+ ($p = \metric{erl.text.p4.h1.block_test.shape.srtm15p.p}$), and is reversed for ETOPO 2022. It is fragile. Without the record-low rule, which removes deep open-shelf water misread as land and so changes the comparison group, \metric{erl.text.redteam.noenv.n_still_significant} of the \metric{erl.text.h1.block.shape.n_significant} remain significant ($p = \metric{erl.text.redteam.noenv.block_p_min}$--\metric{erl.text.redteam.noenv.block_p_max}).

\subsection{ETOPO 2022's apparent skill is post-emergence land data}
ETOPO 2022 mispredicts in only \metric{h1.mispredicts.shape.etopo2022.n_sectors} sector after the shift (in \metric{h1.mispredicts.total.etopo2022.n_sectors} without it), but its skill does not reach the seabed that will emerge next. In the emerged band it takes \metric{h1.etopo2022.source_share.fabdem}\% of its heights from FABDEM, a land elevation model derived from Copernicus radar data acquired while much of that seabed was already dry, and the rest (\metric{h1.etopo2022.source_share.gebco_lake}\%) from GEBCO lake depths\citep{macferrin2025,hawker2022,copdem_handbook_i5}. At levels above \metric{h1.disp_band.edge_2_m}~m its displacement is \metric{erl.text.h1.band_upper.etopo2022_min_km}--\metric{erl.text.h1.band_upper.etopo2022_max_km}~km; below that level it is \metric{erl.text.h1.band_lower.etopo2022_min_km}--\metric{erl.text.h1.band_lower.etopo2022_max_km}~km, within the range of the other grids there (\metric{erl.text.h1.band_lower.others_min_km}--\metric{erl.text.h1.band_lower.others_max_km}~km; figure~\ref{fig:erl_audit}(b)). Independent GEDI heights over the dry seabed show a consistent split: against them, ETOPO 2022's normalised median absolute deviation (NMAD) is \metric{h2.gedi.etopo_src.fabdem.etopo2022.nmad_m}~m where it uses FABDEM and \metric{h2.gedi.etopo_src.gebco_lake.etopo2022.nmad_m}~m where it uses lake depths (Supplementary section~\SIdem). Below the shorelines of the years in which those land data were acquired, which is where the next metres of fall will expose seabed, every audited grid misplaces shorelines by kilometres.

\subsection{The pattern recurs at Great Salt Lake, where a control passes}
At Great Salt Lake every grid mispredicts both arms (\metric{generality.great_salt_lake.n_products_mispredict} of 5 grids, each in \metric{erl.text.gsl.n_arms_mispredicted_min} of 2 arms), with median displacements of \metric{erl.text.gsl.disp_min_m}--\metric{erl.text.gsl.disp_max_m}~m against noise floors of \metric{erl.text.m0.generality.great_salt_lake.north.noise_floor_p95_median_m}~m (north arm) and \metric{erl.text.m0.generality.great_salt_lake.south.noise_floor_p95_median_m}~m (south arm; figure~\ref{fig:erl_gsl}). The lidar control passes the same audit: its median displacement is \metric{erl.text.m0.generality.great_salt_lake.control.north.median_displacement_m}~m and \metric{erl.text.m0.generality.great_salt_lake.control.south.median_displacement_m}~m, above the floor at \metric{generality.great_salt_lake.control.north.n_exceeding_levels} of \metric{generality.great_salt_lake.control.north.n_evaluable_levels} and \metric{generality.great_salt_lake.control.south.n_exceeding_levels} of \metric{generality.great_salt_lake.control.south.n_evaluable_levels} levels, so the audit does not condemn every surface. ETOPO 2022 is again the weakest failure. Its emerged band there is \metric{erl.text.gsl.north.etopo2022_fabdem_pct}\% FABDEM, and in the north arm its displacement is \metric{erl.text.m0.generality.great_salt_lake.etopo_2022.shape.north.median_displacement_above_2013_m}~m above its level of 1~July 2013 and \metric{erl.text.m0.generality.great_salt_lake.etopo_2022.shape.north.median_displacement_below_2013_m}~m below it; it exceeds the floor at only \metric{generality.great_salt_lake.etopo_2022.shape.north.n_exceeding_levels} of \metric{generality.great_salt_lake.etopo_2022.shape.north.n_evaluable_levels} north-arm levels, and with a noise floor pooled over all levels it does not mispredict that arm.

\subsection{Near-term emergence is bathymetry-limited}
For the first metre of fall below the 2026 level, the five grids as users apply them place \metric{erl.text.h3.uncorrected.1.min_km2}~\kmsq{} (90\% interval \metric{erl.text.h3.uncorrected.1.min_lo_km2}--\metric{erl.text.h3.uncorrected.1.min_hi_km2}; \metric{erl.text.h3.uncorrected.1.min_product}) to \metric{erl.text.h3.uncorrected.1.max_km2}~\kmsq{} (\metric{erl.text.h3.uncorrected.1.max_lo_km2}--\metric{erl.text.h3.uncorrected.1.max_hi_km2}; \metric{erl.text.h3.uncorrected.1.max_product}) of seabed in the emerging band, a factor of \metric{erl.text.h3.uncorrected.1.ratio} with intervals that do not overlap; for 2~m the range is \metric{erl.text.h3.uncorrected.2.min_km2} (\metric{erl.text.h3.uncorrected.2.min_lo_km2}--\metric{erl.text.h3.uncorrected.2.min_hi_km2}) to \metric{erl.text.h3.uncorrected.2.max_km2}~\kmsq{} (\metric{erl.text.h3.uncorrected.2.max_lo_km2}--\metric{erl.text.h3.uncorrected.2.max_hi_km2}; figure~\ref{fig:erl_timeline}(b)). They disagree even more on where. At 1~m at least one grid places \metric{erl.agree.1m.area_km2_any}~\kmsq{} in the band, at least three \metric{erl.agree.1m.area_km2_majority}~\kmsq{}, and all five only \metric{erl.agree.1m.area_km2_all}~\kmsq{} (\metric{erl.agree.1m.all_pct_of_any}\%); at 2~m all five agree on \metric{erl.agree.2m.area_km2_all} of \metric{erl.agree.2m.area_km2_any}~\kmsq{} (\metric{erl.agree.2m.all_pct_of_any}\%; figure~\ref{fig:erl_agreement}). Exposure follows the grid: at 1~m, ports, piers and ferry terminals losing the sea within \metric{h3.port_access_km}~km number \metric{erl.text.h3.exposure.ports.1.min_amount} (\metric{erl.text.h3.exposure.ports.1.min_lo}--\metric{erl.text.h3.exposure.ports.1.min_hi}; \metric{erl.text.h3.exposure.ports.1.min_product}) to \metric{erl.text.h3.exposure.ports.1.max_amount} (\metric{erl.text.h3.exposure.ports.1.max_lo}--\metric{erl.text.h3.exposure.ports.1.max_hi}; \metric{erl.text.h3.exposure.ports.1.max_product}), and emerging herbaceous wetland covers \metric{erl.text.h3.exposure.worldcover.1.min_amount}--\metric{erl.text.h3.exposure.worldcover.1.max_amount}~\kmsq{}. The pathways decide when this happens: \metric{erl.text.h3.paths.1m.n_reached} of the \metric{erl.text.h3.paths.n_primary} primary published pathways reach 1~m between \metric{erl.text.h3.paths.1m.first_year} and \metric{erl.text.h3.paths.1m.last_year}, and \metric{erl.text.h3.paths.2m.n_reached} reach 2~m between \metric{erl.text.h3.paths.2m.first_year} and \metric{erl.text.h3.paths.2m.last_year} (figure~\ref{fig:erl_timeline}(a)). The grids decide how much seabed each step exposes, and they do not agree.

Correcting the grids does not resolve this. The registered correction is decision-relevant at 1~m for \metric{erl.text.h3.registered.n_relevant_at_1m} of 5 grids (verdict ``\metric{h3.verdict.etopo2022}'' for ETOPO 2022, ``\metric{h3.verdict.gebco2026}'' for the others) and lowers 1~m emergence by a factor of \metric{erl.text.h3.registered.1.unc_over_fused_min}--\metric{erl.text.h3.registered.1.unc_over_fused_max}. That magnitude comes from a degenerate error model. In \metric{h3.atl24.error_model.n_outside_fit_range} of \metric{h3.atl24.error_model.n_models} grid--sector models, the shift applied in the near-term zone lies outside the shifts the model was fitted on, reaching \metric{h3.atl24.error_model.max_applied_shift_m}~m against fitted shifts of \metric{h3.atl24.error_model.fit_shift_min_m} to \metric{h3.atl24.error_model.fit_shift_max_m}~m, because depths stored in whole metres make the fitted slopes steep. Under the registered constant-shift sensitivity, which stays within the fitted shifts, the correction is decision-relevant at 1~m only for \metric{erl.text.h3.constant.relevant_at_1m_products} (\metric{h3sens.constant.emergence.etopo2022.fused.1.estimate_km2}~\kmsq{}, 90\% interval \metric{h3sens.constant.emergence.etopo2022.fused.1.lo_km2}--\metric{h3sens.constant.emergence.etopo2022.fused.1.hi_km2}), whose emerged band mixes two sources; for the other \metric{erl.text.h3.constant.not_relevant_1m.n_products} grids it changes 1~m emergence by at most \metric{erl.text.h3.constant.not_relevant_1m.max_reldiff_pct}\%. Neither form transfers. Fitted without the lowest epoch and tested on it, the closest analogue to projecting below 2026, the corrected emergence error exceeds the uncorrected one for \metric{erl.text.h3.registered.e3_n_corrected_worse} of 5 grids with the registered form and \metric{erl.text.h3.constant.e3_n_corrected_worse} of 5 with the constant form (Supplementary section~\SInearterm). The pre-registered independent check of the seabed below the 2026 waterline, ICESat-2 nearshore bathymetry (ATL24)\citep{atl24_v2}, could not be made: \metric{h3.atl24.n_granules_extent_in_box} of the \metric{h3.atl24.n_granules_found} granules listed for the domain hold data over the Caspian. We report the registered verdict as registered, but no corrected surface as the better estimate.
